Helical Pipe Heater: Conjugate Heat Transfer

A helical heater modeled from its defining equations and solved as 3D conjugate heat transfer, checked against hand calculations.

Category
Fluid & Thermal
Tools
Ansys Fluent · SolidWorks
Context
Arizona State University

Overview

The pipe geometry was built in SolidWorks from the equations of a helix, then solved in Ansys Fluent with heat flowing from a heated wall into the fluid. The temperature rise predicted by the CFD model was compared with an analytical hand calculation.

Setup and method

  • Geometry: helical curve defined by equations, swept into a 3D pipe in SolidWorks.
  • Case a: water with a 1000 W/m² heated wall, solved at four inlet velocities; temperature rise compared with hand calculation.
  • Case b: glycerin, whose high viscosity produces heating on its own, with no wall heat flux, to isolate viscous heating.
  • Boundary conditions: velocity inlet with varied inlet velocity. Properties held constant except in case b, where viscous heating is switched on.

Results

On the plot of temperature rise against inlet velocity, the CFD and hand-calculation curves lie almost on top of each other, which supports the model setup.
